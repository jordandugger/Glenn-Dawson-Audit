P1
Offer + Positioning: You're Selling to the Wrong Room
Your form describes entrepreneurs, executives and ex-athletes. Your funnels speak to "busy parents 35+" dealing with pain, weight, hormones and (in your words) "autoimmune disorders and stuff like that." Your ads targeted people with a specific pain. That message attracts people looking for relief at the lowest price, which is why calls end in "I can't afford $97." That isn't a price objection. It's a targeting result. Stefan's list converts because it's full of the people your form describes.
Fix: One primary avatar for paid traffic (high-performing professionals 35 to 55 who want to train, travel and perform without breaking down), with the women's body-composition lane run as a separate campaign. Hormones and autoimmune stay in your coaching, not your marketing.
P1
Funnel Economics: The Front End Sells $1 to $129, So Ads Can Never Pay Back
Every cold path ends in a $1 trial ($197/mo after) or a $129 kit. Even a great ad can't be profitable when the first sale is $129 and there's no built-in path up to $497 to $1,297 a month. On top of that, the live webinar has a poor show rate, five funnels run at once with stale dates, and no single number tells you what's working.
Fix: Make the free movement screen the front door and a 6-month program the first sale (Game Plan tab, Step 2). Judge ads by cost per qualified booked call, not cost per lead.
P1
Cash + Numbers: Under One Month of Runway
$3K in reserves, about $29K a month in combined personal and business costs, and accounts that aren't separated. You can't run a disciplined ad test, hire, or invest in growth from a survival position, and decisions get made month to month.
Fix: A 30-day cash campaign built on what already works (JV webinar, past-client win-back, paid-in-full upgrades), separate bank accounts, and a simple weekly numbers tracker.
P2
Meta Compliance: The Current Copy Puts the Ad Account at Risk
"Eliminate chronic pain in 4 weeks," "increase sex hormone production," "fired their chiropractor and improved bloodwork in 30 days," and "complete elimination of pain" are timed health outcomes and medical claims. Meta reviews the ad and the landing page together. These lines can get ads rejected, push up CPMs, or get the account restricted before you ever learn whether the offer works.
Fix: Rewrite every page that receives paid traffic before relaunching ads (the swap table is in the Creative + Compliance tab).
P2
Capacity: Glenn Is the Product, the Marketer and the Bottleneck
Client work all day Monday and Tuesday, 21 personal clients, workouts written during nap time on Saturdays, and Ross covering six roles. More leads would hit a ceiling within weeks. Meanwhile your most valuable credential, Brianna's DPT, is barely visible in the marketing.
Fix: Brianna becomes the face of the movement screen and the authority in the ads. A coach takes over Group delivery. Workout templates and an AI content system give Glenn his Saturdays back.
P3
Organic Profile: A Strong Account with No Clear Next Step
@realglenndawson is verified, with 5,158 followers and 1,044 posts, but the bio link still points to /summer-shred, there's no DM keyword, it follows more accounts than follow it (5,730), most highlights are pets and food, and the pinned posts are physique shots rather than proof or the method. The content mixes physique, recipes, motivation and exercise tips with no clear lane.

OK
Product, Proof and the Assessment: This Is the Engine

Why this fixes "can't afford $97"
  • The qualifying questions filter people before the call, so you stop spending calls on people who were never buyers.
  • The screen is a commitment step. Someone who films themselves squatting is far more serious than someone who registers for a webinar.
  • The value is shown before the price. They see their own movement marked up by a DPT, so $4K for six months feels proportionate.
  • On-demand beats live. No more single-date shows with a poor turnout.
Capacity check
At 2 to 3 minutes per screen, 100 screens a month is about 4 to 5 hours. Split between Glenn and Brianna, it fits easily. It also gives Brianna a visible, front-facing role built around her DPT credential, which ties directly to your goal of giving her the role she wants.
Two entry points, one funnel
Cold traffic: free screen
Ad › free screen (upload + 5 questions) › 10-minute R3 training + calendar › pre-call markup › review call. Cold prospects have never bought from you, so asking for a card on the first visit is where the volume dies. Filming the squat and answering the questions is already the commitment filter. The 10-minute video sits on the thank-you page, after the form, so it lifts show rate without blocking the opt-in.
Warm traffic: paid screen as a deposit
JV webinar, past-client list or your own audience › pitch a $97 Movement Screen + Strategy Review at the end › review call › program. Position it as a deposit, not a product: "applied in full to your program when you enroll." Warm audiences already trust you, so a small paid ask works here and it recovers cost before the call. Keep the Reset Kit out of this path so there's one low-ticket step, not two.
Same screen, same call, same program. Track each path separately: cost per screen, screen-to-call show rate, and close rate. Within 30 days you'll know which entry point produces cheaper qualified calls.
